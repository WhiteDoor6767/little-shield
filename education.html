What Are Child Rights?

Child rights are the fundamental rights and freedoms that all children under the age of 18 are entitled to, regardless of their background, race, gender, or socioeconomic status. These rights are guaranteed by international conventions and national laws to ensure every child can grow up safe, healthy, and with dignity.

In India, children in slum and urban communities are especially vulnerable. They face threats to their survival, development, protection, and participation — the four pillars of child rights recognized by the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC), which India ratified in 1992.

The Four Pillars of Child Rights

Every child has the right to live and have their basic needs met — food, clean water, shelter, clothing, and healthcare.

  • Right to adequate nutrition and clean drinking water
  • Access to basic healthcare and immunization
  • Shelter and protection from extreme weather
  • Protection from disease and preventable death

In slum communities: Many children lack clean water, nutritious food, and access to basic healthcare. Little Shield works to connect families to government schemes and health services.

Children have the right to develop to their fullest potential — mentally, emotionally, physically, and socially.

  • Right to free and compulsory education (Article 21A)
  • Access to play, leisure, and cultural activities
  • Freedom of thought, conscience, and religion
  • Access to information and life-skills development

Did you know? Under India's Right to Education Act (RTE 2009), every child aged 6–14 is entitled to free education in a neighbourhood school.

Children must be shielded from all forms of abuse, neglect, exploitation, and discrimination.

  • Protection from child labour and trafficking
  • Protection from sexual abuse (POCSO Act 2012)
  • Right to a safe home and family environment
  • Protection from violence, torture, and cruel treatment
  • Special protection for children in conflict zones or emergencies
POCSO Act 2012

The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act protects children under 18 from all forms of sexual abuse and exploitation.

Children have the right to be heard and to actively participate in matters that affect their lives.

  • Right to express opinions freely
  • Right to be heard in legal and administrative proceedings
  • Freedom to associate and assemble peacefully
  • Right to access information relevant to their lives

When children are empowered to speak up, communities become stronger and safer for everyone.

⚠️ Warning Signs of Rights Violation

😰

Sudden behaviour changes

Withdrawal, aggression, or fearfulness — especially around certain adults.

🏫

Unexplained school absence

Repeated absenteeism can signal forced labour, abuse, or neglect at home.

🤕

Unexplained injuries

Bruises, burns, or marks with inconsistent explanations from caregivers.

😔

Signs of malnutrition

Severe underweight, lethargy, or poor hygiene can indicate neglect.

💡 Quick Facts

Safety Tips for Children

Simple, powerful rules every child should know to stay safe at home, in school, and in the community.

For Children
🙅

The "No, Go, Tell" Rule

If someone makes you uncomfortable — say NO, GO away from them, and TELL a trusted adult immediately. You will never be in trouble for telling.

For Children
🧠

Your Body Belongs to You

No one has the right to touch the private parts of your body. These are covered by your swimsuit. If anyone tries, it is never your fault.

For Children
🔒

Safe & Unsafe Secrets

A safe secret is a surprise that will soon be shared (like a birthday). An unsafe secret makes you feel scared or bad. Never keep unsafe secrets.

For Children
📍

Know Your Safe Adults

Identify 3 trusted adults (outside your home) you can run to if you feel unsafe — a teacher, neighbour, or community worker.

For Parents & Community
👀

Watch for Grooming Signs

An adult who gives extra gifts, wants to be alone with your child, or asks for secrecy — these are warning signs. Trust your instincts.

For Parents & Community
💬

Keep Communication Open

Create a home where children feel safe to talk about anything without fear of punishment. Regularly ask about their day, friends, and feelings.

For Parents & Community
🏫

Know Your Child's School

Visit the school, know the teachers, and ensure the school has a proper child safety policy and a designated child protection officer.

For Parents & Community
🌐

Online Safety Basics

Teach children to never share personal information online, always tell an adult about uncomfortable online interactions, and keep devices in common areas.

🏘️ Community-Level Safety

Communities can create a "safe spaces map" identifying:

  • Homes of trusted adults children can go to
  • Unsafe or poorly lit areas to avoid after dark
  • Locations of police posts and health centres
  • Community meeting points for emergencies

Neighbourhood watch groups focused on child safety can:

  • Monitor suspicious activity near schools and play areas
  • Ensure children return home safely after school
  • Quickly raise alarms if a child goes missing
  • Coordinate with local police for rapid response

Child Protection Laws in India

Know your rights. These laws are designed to protect every child. Awareness empowers communities to act.

ACT OF 2012

🛡️ POCSO Act

The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act protects children under 18 from all forms of sexual abuse and exploitation.

  • Makes all sexual offences against minors non-bailable
  • Mandatory reporting — failure to report is an offence
  • Child-friendly court procedures to reduce trauma
  • Speedy trial within 1 year
ACT OF 2009

🎓 Right to Education (RTE)

Article 21A and the RTE Act guarantee free and compulsory education to every child aged 6–14 in India.

  • Free education in neighbourhood schools
  • 25% reservation in private schools for economically weaker sections
  • No child can be expelled before Class 8
  • Mid-day meal scheme for nutrition
ACT OF 2016

👶 Juvenile Justice Act

Provides for care, protection, and rehabilitation of children in need and children in conflict with law.

  • Child Welfare Committees (CWC) in every district
  • No child to be held in adult jails
  • Foster care and adoption regulations
  • Special courts for child-related cases
ACT OF 1986 (AMENDED 2016)

🏭 Child Labour Prohibition Act

Prohibits employment of children below 14 years in any occupation and of adolescents (14–18) in hazardous work.

  • Complete ban on child labour below age 14
  • Hazardous occupations banned up to age 18
  • Penalties for employers and parents who violate this law
  • Rescued children entitled to rehabilitation
ACT OF 2000

🚫 IT Act — Online Safety

The Information Technology Act and subsequent amendments protect children from online exploitation and cyber crimes.

  • Criminalises creation and distribution of child sexual abuse material (CSAM)
  • Platforms must report and remove such content immediately
  • Stringent penalties for online grooming and exploitation
NATIONAL COMMISSION

🏛️ NCPCR

The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights monitors and enforces child rights laws across India.

  • Investigates child rights violations
  • Recommends action to governments
  • Accepts complaints from citizens
  • Website: ncpcr.gov.in
Important

How to Report a Child Rights Violation

You do not need to be a lawyer or expert to report a violation. Here's what you can do:

📞

Call Childline

Dial 1098 — free, 24×7 helpline for children in distress. Works across India.

🚔

File an FIR

Visit the nearest police station. Under POCSO, police MUST register an FIR — they cannot refuse.

🏛️

Contact CWC

Every district has a Child Welfare Committee (CWC). Approach them for cases of neglect or need of care.

🌐

Online Complaint

File a complaint on ncpcr.gov.in or the cybercrime.gov.in portal for online abuse cases.

⚖️ Key Legal Numbers

Age 18 — legal age of adulthood for child rights protection

1 year — maximum timeline for POCSO trials to be completed
6–14 years — age bracket for free compulsory education under RTE
